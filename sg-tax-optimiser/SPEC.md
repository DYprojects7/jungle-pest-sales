# Singapore Tax Optimiser — SRS + CPF Contribution Planner

**Product & technical specification, v1.0** · Income year 2026 (Year of Assessment 2027)

> One-line pitch: *Tell me my income, and I'll tell you exactly how much to put into SRS and CPF before
> 31 December to cut my tax bill — and not a dollar more than is worth locking away.*

---

## 1. Problem and positioning

Singapore employees can reduce income tax by making voluntary contributions that earn tax relief:

- **SRS** (Supplementary Retirement Scheme) contributions,
- **CPF cash top-ups** to their own Special/Retirement and MediSave accounts,
- **CPF cash top-ups** to eligible family members.

Existing tools each answer one piece of the question: the IRAS calculator computes tax from reliefs you already
know; bank and robo SRS calculators show "tax saved if you max SRS"; the CPF Board explains top-up relief. None of
them answers the question people actually have in November and December:

> *"Given my income and the reliefs I already have, how much should I put where — and when does putting in more
> stop being worth it?"*

This product is a **tax optimisation calculator**, not just a tax calculator. Its differentiators:

1. **It recommends an allocation** across SRS, CPF (self) and CPF (family), instead of asking the user to guess.
2. **It is bracket-aware.** Every extra dollar of relief saves tax at the user's marginal rate, and that rate steps
   down as chargeable income falls. The tool shows the *savings ladder* and stops at the rung the user chooses.
3. **It never recommends a wasted dollar.** It respects the S$80,000 overall relief cap, each scheme's cap, the
   point where chargeable income becomes tax-free, the user's CPF top-up room and the user's cash budget.
4. **It shows the price of the saving**: the cash locked away, for how long, and the return on it
   ("lock away S$23,300 → save S$2,680 → an immediate 11.5% return").
5. **It turns the answer into a plan**: monthly set-aside amounts up to the 31 December deadline.

### Target users

- Singapore-tax-resident **employees** earning roughly S$80k–S$250k a year (marginal rates 7%–19%), especially
  those with sizeable bonuses.
- Young professionals who have heard "top up SRS/CPF to save tax" but do not know how much or in what order.
- Anyone doing year-end tax planning who wants a defensible number in two minutes.

### Non-goals (v1)

- Not an IRAS filing tool and not financial advice. It estimates; the Notice of Assessment is authoritative.
- **Self-employed** income and MediSave obligations for self-employed persons (different CPF relief rules).
- **Non-residents** for tax purposes (no personal reliefs; different rates).
- Modelling investment returns inside SRS/CPF, or projecting retirement balances.
- Parenthood Tax Rebate balances (a credit carried forward; it changes *when* tax is paid, not the value of a relief
  dollar — see §6.4).
- Employer SRS contributions, NOR scheme, foreign-sourced income, rental-expense modelling.
- Accounts, payments, licence keys or analytics. v1 runs entirely on the user's device.

## 6. Calculation model

All three implementations (TypeScript engine, Python reference, workbook) MUST follow this section exactly.
Symbols in `code` refer to fields in `src/engine/types.ts`.

### 6.0 Conventions and input sanitising

- Money inputs are non-negative whole dollars. Sanitise every numeric input: not finite or `< 0` → `0`; otherwise
  `Math.floor`. `null`/`undefined` optional limits mean "no limit".
- `monthsWorked` is clamped to the integers 1…12 (default 12). `birthMonth` is clamped to 1…12 (default 12).
  `ageAtYearEnd` is clamped to 0…120. `prYear` defaults to 3. `minSavingRate` is clamped to 0…1.
- `priority` must be a permutation of `['srs','cpfSelf','cpfFamily']`; if it is not, missing levers are appended in
  that canonical order and duplicates/unknown values dropped.
- Tax is computed exactly and rounded to cents half-up **once**, at the end of the gross-tax calculation
  (implementation hint: rates have at most one decimal place of a percent, so `rate × 1000` is an integer; do the
  bracket sum in integer "mills" = dollars × rate-per-mille, then `round(mills / 10) / 100`).
- The engine is pure. `today` comes from the input.

### 6.1 Compulsory employee CPF contribution (auto-calculation)

Applies when `residency` is `citizen` or `pr`. For `foreigner` the contribution is 0 (`source: 'none'`).

**Rate table.** `citizen`, or `pr` with `prYear = 3` → `cpf.fullRates`; `pr` with `prYear = 1` → `cpf.prYear1Rates`;
`prYear = 2` → `cpf.prYear2Rates`. (Simplification: the PR year is treated as constant for the whole income year.)

**Months worked.** The employee is assumed to work the *last* `n = monthsWorked` months of the year
(months `13 − n … 12`); other months have zero wages.

**Age band per month.** CPF rates change from the first day of the month *after* the month of the 55th, 60th, 65th
and 70th birthday. For calendar month `m` (1–12):

```
completedAge(m) = ageAtYearEnd − 1   if m ≤ birthMonth
                = ageAtYearEnd       otherwise
band(m)         = first band in the rate table with maxAge === null or completedAge(m) < maxAge
```

**Ordinary wages.** For each worked month, with `ow = monthlyOrdinaryWage` and the month's band:

```
owSubject(m) = min(ow, owCeilingMonthly)
employeeOW(m) = 0                                           if ow ≤ employeeNilUpTo (500)
              = floor(phaseInFactor × (ow − employeeNilUpTo))   if ow ≤ phaseInUpTo (750)
              = floor(employeeRate × owSubject(m))          otherwise
```

**Additional wages.**

```
ordinaryWagesSubject  = Σ owSubject(m) over worked months
additionalWageCeiling = max(0, annualWageCeiling − ordinaryWagesSubject)
additionalWagesSubject = min(additionalWages, additionalWageCeiling)
employeeAW = floor(employeeRate(band(12)) × additionalWagesSubject)
```

(Additional wages are assumed to be paid in December for age-band purposes, and to attract the full employee rate.)

**Result.**

```
employeeContribution = Σ employeeOW(m) + employeeAW
employerContribution = round(Σ employerRate(band(m)) × owSubject(m) + employerRate(band(12)) × additionalWagesSubject)   (informational)
monthlyEmployeeRates[m−1] = employeeRate(band(m)) for worked months, else 0
```

**Override.** If `income.employeeCpfOverride` is a number, `employeeContribution = min(override, maxCompulsory)`
with `maxCompulsory = floor(fullRates[0].employeeRate × annualWageCeiling)`; `source: 'override'`; the wage
breakdown fields are still reported from the auto-calculation; `employerContribution = 0`. If the override exceeded
`maxCompulsory`, emit `CPF_OVERRIDE_ABOVE_MAX`. An override is ignored for foreigners only if it is `null`/absent —
a foreigner with an explicit override keeps it (covers foreigners with a voluntary employer arrangement? no:
**foreigners always get 0**; the override is ignored and `source` stays `'none'`).

**Known approximations (documented in the UI):** contributions are computed month by month on ordinary wages with
the employee share rounded down each month; real payslips combine OW and AW in the month paid and can differ by a
few dollars. The override exists so users can enter the exact figure from payslips or the CPF statement.

### 6.2 Income

```
employmentIncome   = monthlyOrdinaryWage × monthsWorked + additionalWages
totalIncome        = max(0, employmentIncome − employmentExpenses) + otherTaxableIncome
donationDeduction  = donations × donationDeductionMultiplier
assessableIncome   = max(0, totalIncome − donationDeduction)
```

### 6.3 Reliefs and the overall cap

For existing contributions `e` and additional contributions `a` (per lever), with the lever's statutory cap for the
profile (§6.5):

```
earnedIncome   = min(EIR(ageAtYearEnd), max(0, employmentIncome − employmentExpenses))
                 EIR: below55 if age < 55; age55to59 if 55 ≤ age ≤ 59; age60plus if age ≥ 60
cpf            = employeeContribution (§6.1)
srs            = min(e.srs + a.srs, statutoryCap.srs)
cpfTopUpSelf   = min(e.cpfTopUpSelf + a.cpfSelf, statutoryCap.cpfSelf)
cpfTopUpFamily = min(e.cpfTopUpFamily + a.cpfFamily, statutoryCap.cpfFamily)
other          = otherReliefs
totalBeforeCap = sum of the six
totalAllowed   = min(totalBeforeCap, personalReliefCap)
lostToCap      = totalBeforeCap − totalAllowed
chargeableIncome = floor(max(0, assessableIncome − totalAllowed))
```

### 6.4 Tax

```
grossTax(ci)  = round2( Σ over brackets k:  rate_k × max(0, min(ci, from_{k+1}) − from_k) )      (from_{last+1} = ∞)
pitRebate     = 0 if rules.pitRebate is null, else min(cap, round2(rate × grossTax))
taxPayable    = max(0, grossTax − pitRebate)
marginalRate  = 0 if ci = 0, else the rate of the bracket with the largest `from` < ci
effectiveRate = taxPayable / totalIncome   (0 when totalIncome = 0)
```

`evaluate(input, additional)` returns the full `TaxComputation` for the profile with `additional` contributions on
top of `existing`. `baseline = evaluate(input, {0,0,0})`.

*Parenthood Tax Rebate.* PTR is a credit that offsets tax payable and carries forward until used. A dollar of gross
tax avoided therefore preserves a dollar of PTR for later years, so PTR does not change the value of a relief
dollar. v1 does not ask for a PTR balance; the UI notes that a user with unused PTR will pay less than shown this
year and that the saving is realised when the PTR balance would otherwise have run out.

### 6.5 Levers: eligibility, caps and room

| Lever | Statutory relief cap (`statutoryCap`) | Eligible when |
|-------|----------------------------------------|---------------|
| `srs` | `srs.capCitizenPr` for citizen/PR; `srs.capForeigner` for foreigner | `ageAtYearEnd ≥ 18` |
| `cpfSelf` | `cpfTopUp.selfReliefCap` | citizen or PR (has a CPF account) |
| `cpfFamily` | `cpfTopUp.familyReliefCap` | always (the recipient must be an eligible family member with a CPF account) |

For an ineligible lever `statutoryCap = 0`. Then, per lever:

```
used = min(existing, statutoryCap)
roomIfEnabled = max(0, statutoryCap − used)
   cpfSelf:   roomIfEnabled = min(roomIfEnabled, levers.cpfSelf.roomToLimit)   when roomToLimit is a number
   cpfFamily: roomIfEnabled = min(roomIfEnabled, levers.cpfFamily.maxAmount)   when maxAmount is a number
room = roomIfEnabled if (eligible and enabled) else 0
```

`EXISTING_EXCEEDS_CAP` is emitted when an existing contribution is larger than a non-zero statutory cap.

### 6.6 The optimiser

Because every relief dollar reduces chargeable income by exactly one dollar whichever scheme it goes into, the tax
saved depends only on the **total** additional relief. The optimisation therefore separates into (1) *how much in
total* and (2) *which scheme first*.

Let `CI0 = baseline.chargeableIncome`, `N(x)` = `taxPayable` after `x` dollars of additional allowed relief.

```
reliefCapRoom = max(0, personalReliefCap − baseline.reliefs.totalBeforeCap)                      (C)
floorCI(r)    = `from` of the lowest bracket whose rate > 0 and rate ≥ r  (∞ if none)
usefulRoom(r) = max(0, CI0 − floorCI(r))                                                         (U = usefulRoom(minSavingRate))
leverRoom     = Σ room over the three levers                                                     (L)
budget        = cashBudget if it is a number, else ∞                                             (B)

X = min(U, C, L, B)                      total additional contribution (a whole number of dollars)
```

Allocation: walk `preferences.priority`; give each lever `min(room, remaining)`.

```
plan.taxBefore = baseline.taxPayable;  plan.taxAfter = evaluate(input, contributions).taxPayable
plan.taxSaved  = round2(taxBefore − taxAfter);  plan.savingRate = taxSaved / X  (0 if X = 0)
```

**Binding constraint.** `'none'` if `baseline.taxPayable = 0`. Otherwise the constraint with the smallest value among
`U, C, L, B`, ties resolved in the order `usefulRoom`, `reliefCap`, `leverLimits`, `cashBudget`.

**Unrealised saving.** `taxPayable(after X) − taxPayable(after min(U, C))`, i.e. the additional tax that could still be
saved at or above the user's minimum rate if the schemes or the budget had more room. It is 0 when the binding
constraint is `usefulRoom`, `reliefCap` or `none`.

**Why this is optimal.** Tax payable is a convex, piecewise-linear, non-decreasing function of chargeable income, so
the saving from each successive relief dollar is non-increasing. Taking dollars in order until the first one that
fails a constraint is therefore optimal for "maximise tax saved subject to: each dollar saves at least
`minSavingRate`, total ≤ budget, per-scheme limits, relief cap". The order of schemes does not change the tax
result; it only expresses the user's preference between flexibility and guaranteed interest (see §8.3).

**Statutory-rate note.** `minSavingRate` is compared with statutory bracket rates. In a year with a percentage PIT
rebate, the realised saving on low tax bills can be smaller than the bracket rate; `taxSaved` always reports the
realised figure and `PIT_REBATE_APPLIED` is emitted.

### 6.7 Savings ladder

The ladder partitions the range `[0, L]` of possible additional contributions (all enabled levers at their rooms,
ignoring budget and minimum rate) into tiers of constant saving rate, in the order the dollars would be contributed:

1. Dollars `[0, min(L, C))` reduce chargeable income from `CI0` downwards. Split this range at every bracket
   boundary crossed. Each piece is a tier with `rate` = that bracket's rate. Pieces in the 0% bracket (including
   dollars beyond `CI0`) have `rate = 0`, `zeroReason = 'belowTaxThreshold'`.
2. Dollars `[min(L, C), L)` exceed the relief cap: one tier with `rate = 0`, `zeroReason = 'reliefCap'`.

Adjacent tiers with the same `rate` and `zeroReason` are merged; empty tiers are dropped.
`taxSaved = N(fromContribution) − N(toContribution)` (realised, so it reflects any PIT rebate),
`cumulativeTaxSaved = N(0) − N(toContribution)`, `usedByPlan = overlap([from, to), [0, X))`.

### 6.8 Simulator ("every extra S$1,000")

Step `s = preferences.simulatorStep` (default 1,000; minimum 100). Rows for contributions
`c_i = min(i × s, L)`, `i = 0 … ceil(L / s)`. For each row: `split` = allocation of `c_i` across enabled levers in
priority order (ignoring budget and minimum rate); `taxPayable`, `taxSaved = baseline − taxPayable`;
`stepSaving = taxSaved_i − taxSaved_{i−1}`; `stepRate = stepSaving / (c_i − c_{i−1})`;
`withinPlan = c_i ≤ X`. Row 0 has `stepSaving = stepRate = 0`.

### 6.9 Scenarios

Fixed comparisons on the same inputs. They ignore budget, minimum rate, priority and the `enabled` flags of `srs`
and `cpfSelf` (those are preferences) but respect eligibility, existing contributions and the user's room limits.
`cpfFamily` is included only when it is enabled (it depends on having eligible family members).

| id | Contributions |
|----|---------------|
| `none` | nothing |
| `srsOnly` | `srs = roomIfEnabled(srs)` |
| `cpfSelfOnly` | `cpfSelf = roomIfEnabled(cpfSelf)` |
| `srsPlusCpfSelf` | both of the above |
| `maxAll` | both of the above, plus `cpfFamily = room(cpfFamily)` |
| `recommended` | the plan from §6.6 |

For each: `taxPayable`, `taxSaved`, `savingRate = taxSaved / total`, and
`wastedContribution = total − min(total, usefulRoom(0), C)` — dollars that buy no tax reduction.
(For an ineligible lever `roomIfEnabled = 0`.)

### 6.10 Monthly plan

```
deadline        = rules.contributionDeadline (31 December of the income year)
daysToDeadline  = whole days from today to deadline (negative if passed)
monthsRemaining = 12                         if today's year < incomeYear
                = 12 − month(today) + 1      if today's year = incomeYear   (current month counts)
                = 0                          if today's year > incomeYear
```

Schedule (when `monthsRemaining = n > 0` and `X > 0`): `n` rows from the first counted month to December. For each
lever with amount `x`: `base = floor(x / n)`, `extra = x − base × n`; the first `extra` rows get `base + 1`, the rest
`base` (whole dollars that sum exactly to `x`). `fullYearMonthly[lever] = ceil(x / 12)`, `total` = their sum.

### 6.11 Warnings

| Code | Severity | Condition |
|------|----------|-----------|
| `NO_TAX_PAYABLE` | info | `baseline.taxPayable = 0` |
| `RELIEF_CAP_EXCEEDED_BASELINE` | caution | `baseline.reliefs.lostToCap > 0` |
| `RELIEF_CAP_LIMITS_PLAN` | caution | binding constraint is `reliefCap` |
| `BELOW_MIN_SAVING_RATE` | info | `baseline.taxPayable > 0` and `U = 0` |
| `BUDGET_LIMITS_PLAN` | info | binding constraint is `cashBudget` |
| `LEVER_LIMITS_PLAN` | info | binding constraint is `leverLimits` |
| `EXISTING_EXCEEDS_CAP` | caution | an existing contribution exceeds its statutory cap |
| `CPF_OVERRIDE_ABOVE_MAX` | caution | override above the maximum compulsory contribution |
| `FOREIGNER_NO_CPF` | info | `residency = 'foreigner'` |
| `PR_GRADUATED_RATES` | info | `residency = 'pr'` and `prYear < 3` |
| `AGE_55_PLUS_TOPUP` | info | `ageAtYearEnd ≥ 55` and `cpfSelf` enabled and eligible |
| `SRS_UNDER_18` | caution | `ageAtYearEnd < 18` |
| `DEADLINE_PASSED` | blocker | `daysToDeadline < 0` |
| `DEADLINE_SOON` | caution | `0 ≤ daysToDeadline ≤ 14` and `X > 0` |
| `PROJECTED_RULES` | caution | `rules.status = 'projected'` |
| `PIT_REBATE_APPLIED` | info | `baseline.pitRebate > 0` |

Each warning carries a ready-to-display `message` and machine-readable `params`.

## 8. Web calculator

### 8.1 Principles

- **Answer first.** The recommendation and the tax saved are visible without scrolling on desktop and directly
  under a compact input card on phones. Results update on every keystroke; there is no "Calculate" button.
- **Opens in a working state** with the worked example, labelled as an example, so the first view shows what the
  tool does.
- **Show the working.** Every headline number can be traced to a line in the tax computation panel.
- **Plain language.** "Cash you lock away", not "illiquidity"; "tax saved per dollar", not "marginal rate" (the
  term is introduced once, next to the ladder).
- **Private by construction.** No network requests, no analytics, no cookies. Inputs persist only in the browser's
  `localStorage` (wrapped in try/catch; the app works without it).
- **Not IRAS, and says so.** A persistent, unobtrusive "Estimate only — not tax advice" line with the YA.

### 8.2 Layout

Desktop (≥ 960 px): two columns — inputs on the left (scrolls), results on the right. Phone: one column in the order
*inputs summary → recommendation → ladder → plan details*, with a sticky bottom bar showing
"Save S$X · lock away S$Y" that scrolls to the recommendation when tapped.

### 8.3 Inputs

1. **About you** — residency (Citizen / PR / Foreigner tax resident); PR year (1st / 2nd / 3rd+) when PR; age at
   31 Dec of the income year; birth month (only shown when the age is 55, 60, 65 or 70); income year selector
   (shown only when more than one year is supported; projected years are flagged).
2. **Income** — pay entry as monthly or annual salary (toggle); bonus and other additional wages for the year;
   months worked (default 12). *More options* (collapsed): other taxable income, employment expenses, donations,
   "Use my actual employee CPF for the year" (override).
3. **Already contributed this year** — SRS, CPF top-up to self, CPF top-up to family.
4. **Other reliefs** — one total field, or *itemise* with the relief helper built from `rules.reliefCatalog`
   (spouse, children, parents, NSman, life insurance, other). The helper only sums amounts; it does not check
   eligibility and says so.
5. **Your preferences**
   - *Schemes to use*: SRS ✓, CPF top-up (self) ✓, CPF top-up (family) ☐. Per-scheme optional limits:
     "room left before the Full Retirement Sum / Basic Healthcare Sum" for self; "most I would give" for family.
   - *How far down the ladder?* A segmented control built from the bracket rates at or below the user's current
     marginal rate: **Worth it (7%+)** *(default)*, **Top bracket only**, **Every tax dollar**, or a custom minimum.
   - *Cash limit*: optional maximum to lock away this year.
   - *Which first?* **Flexibility first** (SRS → CPF self → family) *(default)*, **Guaranteed interest first**
     (CPF self → SRS → family), **Family first** (family → SRS → CPF self). One-line explanation of each.

Numeric fields accept digits and thousands separators, show an "S$" prefix, select-all on focus, and clamp to their
legal range with an inline explanation (e.g. "SRS is capped at S$15,300 a year").

### 8.4 Results

1. **Recommendation card.** "Put **S$A** into SRS and **S$B** into CPF. You save **S$Z** in tax."
   Sub-line: tax before → after; cash locked away; the immediate return ("11.5 cents back per dollar").
   A one-sentence reason the plan stops where it does, driven by `bindingConstraint`
   (e.g. "Beyond this, each extra dollar would save 3.5 cents or less." / "You have used every scheme's limit;
   S$12,400 of your income is still taxed at 11.5%." / "You have reached the S$80,000 overall relief cap.").
   When the recommendation is S$0, the card explains why and offers the *Every tax dollar* view.
2. **Savings ladder.** A stepped chart: x = additional contribution, y = tax saved per dollar; each tier a block
   whose area is the tax saved; the recommended range filled, the rest outlined; zero-saving tiers hatched and
   labelled ("no tax saved: income already tax-free" / "over the S$80,000 relief cap"). Below it, the same data as
   a table (tier, amount, rate, tax saved, cumulative).
3. **Compare options.** Scenario table from §6.9: contribution split, cash locked away, tax, tax saved, saved per
   dollar, wasted dollars (highlighted when > 0). The recommended row is marked.
4. **Monthly plan.** Schedule to December per scheme; days to the deadline; "start in January" monthly figure;
   deadline cautions (bank cut-offs).
5. **Every extra S$1,000.** Simulator table (collapsed by default) with step selector.
6. **How your tax is worked out.** Income → deductions → each relief → chargeable income → bracket-by-bracket tax,
   before and after the plan, side by side.
7. **Before you transfer.** The lock-up facts for each scheme (see §5), eligibility cautions, engine warnings.
8. **Sources and assumptions.** Rule values with links; what the tool does not model; the disclaimer.

A **Copy summary** button copies a plain-text summary of the plan (clipboard write inside the click handler, with
a select-the-text fallback). **Reset to example** restores the opening state.

### 8.5 Design and accessibility

- Theme tokens on `:root`, dark values under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` guarded by
  `:root:not([data-theme="light"])` and again under `:root[data-theme="dark"]`; `body` has an explicit background.
  No colour is defined only inside a theme block.
- Side gutters `max(16px, env(safe-area-inset-left/right))`; no horizontal page scroll at 360 px; tables scroll in
  their own containers; `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` for all figures.
- Every control has a label and a stable `id`; results region is an `aria-live="polite"` region; charts have a text
  alternative (the table); visible focus states; `prefers-reduced-motion` respected; colour is never the only
  signal (hatching/labels on the ladder).
- No external requests except an optional Google Fonts stylesheet with a full system-font fallback stack.
- Visual direction: a calm, precise financial instrument — not a bank advert and not a government form. It must
  not imitate IRAS/CPF branding.

### 8.6 Build

`npm run build` → `dist/index.html`, one file with all CSS/JS inlined (Vite + `vite-plugin-singlefile`, Preact).
`npm run build:release` copies it to `release/sg-tax-optimiser.html`.

## 9. Workbook (Excel / Google Sheets)

Generated by `npm run build:workbook` (`workbook/build.ts`, ExcelJS) from the same `YearRules` data.

- **Compatibility:** Excel 2016+, Google Sheets, LibreOffice. No macros, no `LET`/`LAMBDA`/`XLOOKUP`/dynamic
  arrays; only functions such as `SUMPRODUCT`, `INDEX`, `MATCH`, `MIN`, `MAX`, `IF`, `FLOOR`, `ROUND`, `CHOOSE`.
- **Sheets**
  1. `Start` — what it is, three-step instructions, disclaimer, version and YA.
  2. `Planner` — inputs (clearly styled input cells with data validation and dropdowns) on the left; the
     recommendation, tax before/after, saving, binding-constraint sentence and warnings on the right.
  3. `Ladder` — the savings ladder tiers and a chart.
  4. `Scenarios` — the scenario comparison.
  5. `Simulator` — tax and saving for every extra step (default S$1,000) up to the maximum contribution.
  6. `Monthly Plan` — instalment schedule to December from an "as at" date (default `=TODAY()`).
  7. `Tax Working` — the full computation, baseline and optimised side by side.
  8. `Rules` — the rate table, CPF rates and ceilings, caps, deadline, sources. Editable so a buyer can update it for a
     new year.
- **Parity:** for the same inputs the workbook's CPF contribution, chargeable income, tax before/after, recommended
  amounts per scheme and tax saved equal the engine's (to the cent).
- **Verification:** `tests/workbook.test.ts` writes a set of input profiles into the workbook, recalculates each
  with headless LibreOffice, reads the results back and compares them with `plan()`. The test is skipped with a
  clear message when `soffice` is not installed.

## 10. Verification strategy

| Layer | What it proves | Where |
|-------|----------------|-------|
| Unit tests | Each module matches §6 on hand-computed cases and edge cases | `tests/*.test.ts` |
| Official worked examples | Engine reproduces IRAS / CPF Board published numbers | `tests/golden.test.ts`, `tests/fixtures/` |
| Property tests (fast-check) | Invariants hold for arbitrary inputs: tax is monotone in income; plan respects every cap, room and budget; no recommended dollar saves less than the minimum rate; one more dollar would either break a constraint or save less than the minimum; `recommended` saves at least as much as any feasible allocation found by brute force on small grids; ladder and simulator totals reconcile with `plan` | `tests/properties.test.ts` |
| Differential test | An independent Python implementation written only from this spec agrees with the engine on thousands of random inputs | `reference/sgtax_reference.py`, `tests/differential.test.ts` |
| Workbook parity | Spreadsheet formulas equal the engine | `tests/workbook.test.ts` |
| Browser end-to-end | The built single file loads with no console errors, shows the engine's numbers for the example, reacts to input, and has no horizontal overflow at phone width in both themes | `tests/e2e/` (Playwright) |

`npm test` runs everything except the browser test; `npm run test:e2e` runs the browser test against `dist/index.html`.

## 11. Disclaimers and compliance

- Shown in the UI, the workbook and the README: *"This is an estimate for planning, not tax, legal or financial
  advice. It is not affiliated with IRAS or the CPF Board. Check your eligibility and limits with IRAS, the CPF
  Board and your SRS bank before transferring money. CPF top-ups cannot be reversed."*
- The tool never asks for NRIC, name or account numbers.
- Recommending SRS/CPF contributions is general information about tax relief, not a recommendation of an
  investment product; the copy avoids "you should invest in…" and avoids promising returns.

## 13. Updating for a new year

1. Copy `src/engine/rules/2026.ts` to the new year and update the values (tax table, CPF ceilings and rates,
   retirement sums, BHS, caps, deadline, sources); mark `status: 'projected'` until every value is confirmed.
2. Register it in `src/engine/rules/index.ts`.
3. Mirror the values in `reference/sgtax_reference.py` (it holds its own copy on purpose).
4. Run `npm test` and `npm run build:workbook`.
