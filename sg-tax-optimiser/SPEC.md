# Singapore Tax Optimiser — SRS + CPF Contribution Planner

**Product & technical specification, v1.0** · Income year 2026 (Year of Assessment 2027)

> One-line pitch: *Tell me my income, and I'll tell you exactly how much to put into SRS and CPF before
> 31 December to cut my tax bill — and not a dollar more than is worth locking away.*

---

## 1. Problem and positioning

Singapore employees can reduce income tax by making voluntary contributions that earn tax relief:

- **SRS** (Supplementary Retirement Scheme) contributions,
- **CPF cash top-ups** to their own Special/Retirement and MediSave accounts,
- **CPF cash top-ups** to eligible family members.

Existing tools each answer one piece of the question: the IRAS calculator computes tax from reliefs you already
know; bank and robo SRS calculators show "tax saved if you max SRS"; the CPF Board explains top-up relief. None of
them answers the question people actually have in November and December:

> *"Given my income and the reliefs I already have, how much should I put where — and when does putting in more
> stop being worth it?"*

This product is a **tax optimisation calculator**, not just a tax calculator. Its differentiators:

1. **It recommends an allocation** across SRS, CPF (self) and CPF (family), instead of asking the user to guess.
2. **It is bracket-aware.** Every extra dollar of relief saves tax at the user's marginal rate, and that rate steps
   down as chargeable income falls. The tool shows the *savings ladder* and stops at the rung the user chooses.
3. **It never recommends a dollar it can see would be wasted.** It respects the S$80,000 overall relief cap, each
   scheme's cap, the point where chargeable income becomes tax-free, the CPF top-up room the user enters and the
   user's cash budget. It cannot see CPF balances, government matching grants or a family member's eligibility, so
   it says so, asks for the room where a wrong guess would cost money (§6.5, age 55 and over) and raises a caution
   wherever a recommended dollar depends on something only the user can check (§6.11).
4. **It shows the price of the saving**: the cash locked away, for how long, and the return on it
   ("lock away S$23,300 → save S$2,680 → an immediate 11.5% return").
5. **It turns the answer into a plan**: monthly set-aside amounts up to the 31 December deadline.

### Target users

- Singapore-tax-resident **employees** earning roughly S$80k–S$250k a year (marginal rates 7%–19%), especially
  those with sizeable bonuses.
- Young professionals who have heard "top up SRS/CPF to save tax" but do not know how much or in what order.
- Anyone doing year-end tax planning who wants a defensible number in two minutes.

### Non-goals (v1)

- Not an IRAS filing tool and not financial advice. It estimates; the Notice of Assessment is authoritative.
- **Self-employed** income and MediSave obligations for self-employed persons (different CPF relief rules).
- **Non-residents** for tax purposes (no personal reliefs; different rates).
- Modelling investment returns inside SRS/CPF, or projecting retirement balances.
- Parenthood Tax Rebate balances (a credit carried forward; it changes *when* tax is paid, not the value of a relief
  dollar — see §6.4). The user can tick "I have unused Parenthood Tax Rebate" to be told this (§6.11).
- The tax on SRS **withdrawals**, which depends on the person's income and tax residency decades from now. The
  rules are stated (§5.4), and a foreigner — who is likely to withdraw as a non-resident — gets a caution and a
  more conservative default (§6.11, §8.3).
- Employer SRS contributions, NOR scheme, foreign-sourced income, rental-expense modelling.
- Accounts, payments, licence keys or analytics. v1 runs entirely on the user's device.

## 3. User stories

1. *As an employee*, I enter my salary, bonus and age and immediately see my estimated tax for YA2027.
2. *As a planner*, I see a recommended SRS / CPF split, the tax it saves, and the cash it locks away.
3. *As a sceptic*, I see the savings ladder: "the first S$X saves 11.5 cents per dollar, the next S$Y saves 7 cents",
   and choose where to stop.
4. *As someone with limited spare cash*, I set a budget and the plan fits inside it.
5. *As someone who already contributed this year*, I enter what I have done and the plan only recommends the rest.
6. *As a comparer*, I see ready-made scenarios (SRS only, CPF only, both, everything) side by side with my plan.
7. *As a procrastinator*, I get a monthly set-aside schedule to the deadline, and a "start in January" figure for
   next year.
8. *As a careful person*, I can expand the full tax computation and the rule sources and check every line.
9. *As a spreadsheet person*, I get the same model as an Excel/Google Sheets workbook I can keep and edit.

### 5.2 Reliefs computed by the tool

| Relief | Rule |
|---|---|
| Overall cap | Total personal reliefs are capped at **S$80,000** per YA. The cap includes compulsory CPF relief, Earned Income Relief, SRS relief and CPF cash top-up relief. Relief above the cap is lost: it cannot be carried forward, and the SRS/CPF money cannot be refunded. Donation deductions and employment expenses are not personal reliefs and sit outside the cap. |
| Earned Income Relief | S$1,000 (below 55), S$6,000 (55–59), S$8,000 (60 and above), by age on 31 December of the income year; limited to earned income. |
| CPF relief (employees) | The compulsory employee contribution on Ordinary Wages (up to the OW ceiling) and Additional Wages (up to the AW ceiling). Voluntary contributions earn no relief. |
| Donations | Qualifying donations are deducted at 250% in arriving at assessable income (the 250% rate was extended to donations made up to 31 December 2029). |

  `factor = 3 × employee rate` (0.6 for 55 and below), so it meets the full rate exactly at S$750.
- Rounding: the total contribution is rounded to the nearest dollar; the employee share drops the cents.
- A new age band applies from the first day of the month after the month of the 55th/60th/65th/70th birthday.
- SPRs in their first and second year pay graduated employee rates (the G/G and F/G options share the same

- Maximum compulsory employee contribution for someone 55 or below: 20% × S$102,000 = **S$20,400**.

**Tax residency.** The tool is for tax residents. A foreigner is a tax resident for a Year of Assessment if they
stayed or worked in Singapore for at least 183 days in the calendar year before it (IRAS also accepts a stay of 183
days in a row across two years, and a work pass valid for at least a year). A non-resident gets no personal
reliefs and no SRS relief, and pays 15% or the resident rates on employment income, whichever is higher.

The user enters other reliefs as a total, or builds the total with a helper that offers the common ones:
Spouse Relief S$2,000; Qualifying Child Relief S$4,000 per child; Working Mother's Child Relief (entered as an
amount: S$8,000 / S$10,000 / S$12,000 for the 1st / 2nd / 3rd+ child born from 2024, or 15% / 20% / 25% of earned

two; NSman Self Relief S$1,500 / S$3,000 / S$3,500 / S$5,000; NSman Wife/Parent Relief S$750; Grandparent Caregiver
Relief S$3,000; the disability variants (Spouse S$5,500, Child S$7,500, Parent S$14,000 / S$10,000, Sibling

- **YA2027 rebate:** unknown until Budget 2027; assumed nil.

## 6. Calculation model

All three implementations (TypeScript engine, Python reference, workbook) MUST follow this section exactly.
Symbols in `code` refer to fields in `src/engine/types.ts`.

### 6.0 Conventions and input sanitising

- Money inputs are non-negative whole dollars. Sanitise every numeric input: not finite or `< 0` → `0`; otherwise
  `Math.floor`. `null`/`undefined` optional limits mean "no limit".
- `monthsWorked` is clamped to the integers 1…12 (default 12). `birthMonth` is clamped to 1…12 (default 12).
  `ageAtYearEnd` is clamped to 0…120. `prYear` defaults to 3. `minSavingRate` is clamped to 0…1.
- `priority` must be a permutation of `['srs','cpfSelf','cpfFamily']`; if it is not, missing levers are appended in
  that canonical order and duplicates/unknown values dropped.
- Tax is computed exactly and rounded to cents half-up **once**, at the end of the gross-tax calculation
  (implementation hint: rates have at most one decimal place of a percent, so `rate × 1000` is an integer; do the
  bracket sum in integer "mills" = dollars × rate-per-mille, then `round(mills / 10) / 100`).
- Money inputs are also clamped to at most S$10,000,000,000 so that integer arithmetic stays exact.
- Numeric fields that are clamped (`monthsWorked`, `birthMonth`, `ageAtYearEnd`, `simulatorStep`) are first
  sanitised like money (not finite or `< 0` → 0, then floored) and then clamped; a missing or non-number value takes
  the default (12, 12, 0, 1,000). `prYear` is 3 unless it is exactly 1 or 2. An unknown `residency` is treated as
  `citizen`. Optional limits (`cashBudget`, `roomToLimit`, `maxAmount`) apply only when they
  are numbers (then sanitised like money, so a negative limit is 0); `null`, missing or non-number means no limit.
  `employeeCpfOverride` is not a limit but a figure that replaces a calculated one, so reading a meaningless value
  as 0 would not be cautious (it would remove the CPF relief, overstate the tax and over-recommend): it applies only
  when it is a finite number of 0 or more (then sanitised like money); `NaN`, `±Infinity`, a negative number, `null`,
  missing or a non-number all mean "not provided", and the auto-calculation of §6.1 is used.
  `minSavingRate` is clamped to 0…1 without flooring (not a number → 0).
- `NaN` and `±Infinity` are numbers that are not finite: in `monthsWorked`, `birthMonth`, `ageAtYearEnd` and
  `simulatorStep` they become 0 and are then clamped (so `monthsWorked: NaN` is 1, not the default 12); as an
  optional limit they are a limit of 0 (so `cashBudget: Infinity` recommends nothing; "no limit" is `null` or a
  missing field); as `employeeCpfOverride` they mean "not provided"; in `minSavingRate`, `NaN` is 0 and `±Infinity` is clamped like any other number (to 1 and 0).
- A lever is enabled only when its `enabled` flag is exactly the boolean `true`; any other value (missing, `1`,
  `"true"`) means not enabled, so nothing is ever recommended for a scheme that was not explicitly switched on.
  `profile.hasUnusedParenthoodTaxRebate` is likewise `true` only for the boolean `true`, otherwise `false`.
- `today` is read as its leading `YYYY-MM-DD`. If it cannot be parsed, 1 January of the income year is used.
  It can be parsed only when it is a string that starts — with no leading whitespace — with exactly four digits,
  a hyphen, two digits, a hyphen and two digits (ASCII digits) which name a real date of the proleptic Gregorian
  calendar (year `0000` included), not followed directly by another digit; whatever comes after (a time, a zone)
  is ignored. So `2026-02-30`, `2026-13-01`, `2026-1-5`, ` 2026-11-13` and `2026-11-135` cannot be parsed.
- The engine is pure. `today` comes from the input.

### 6.1 Compulsory employee CPF contribution (auto-calculation)

Applies when `residency` is `citizen` or `pr`. For `foreigner` the contribution is 0 (`source: 'none'`) and every
other field of the CPF computation is 0 (twelve zero monthly rates).

**Rate table.** `citizen`, or `pr` with `prYear = 3` → `cpf.fullRates`; `pr` with `prYear = 1` → `cpf.prYear1Rates`;
`prYear = 2` → `cpf.prYear2Rates`. (Simplification: the PR year is treated as constant for the whole income year.)

**Months worked.** The employee is assumed to work the *last* `n = monthsWorked` months of the year
(months `13 − n … 12`); other months have zero wages.

**Age band per month.** CPF rates change from the first day of the month *after* the month of the 55th, 60th, 65th
and 70th birthday. For calendar month `m` (1–12):

```
completedAge(m) = ageAtYearEnd − 1   if m ≤ birthMonth
                = ageAtYearEnd       otherwise
band(m)         = first band in the rate table with maxAge === null or completedAge(m) < maxAge
```

**Ordinary wages.** For each worked month, with `ow = monthlyOrdinaryWage` and the month's band:

```
owSubject(m) = min(ow, owCeilingMonthly)
employeeOW(m) = 0                                           if ow ≤ employeeNilUpTo (500)
              = floor(phaseInFactor × (ow − employeeNilUpTo))   if ow ≤ phaseInUpTo (750)
              = floor(employeeRate × owSubject(m))          otherwise
```

**Additional wages.**

```
ordinaryWagesSubject  = Σ owSubject(m) over worked months
additionalWageCeiling = max(0, annualWageCeiling − ordinaryWagesSubject)
additionalWagesSubject = min(additionalWages, additionalWageCeiling)
employeeAW = floor(employeeRate(band(12)) × additionalWagesSubject)
```

(Additional wages are assumed to be paid in December for age-band purposes, and to attract the full employee rate.)

**Result.**

```
employeeContribution = Σ employeeOW(m) + employeeAW
employerContribution = round(Σ employerRate(band(m)) × owSubject(m) + employerRate(band(12)) × additionalWagesSubject)   (informational)
monthlyEmployeeRates[m−1] = employeeRate(band(m)) for worked months, else 0      (the band's full rate, even for low wages)
```

`round` for the employer figure is half-up to the nearest dollar. The reference implementation reports
`employerContribution = 0` for first- and second-year SPRs; the field is informational and is not compared.

**Override.** If `income.employeeCpfOverride` is provided (§6.0: a finite number of 0 or more), `employeeContribution = min(override, maxCompulsory)`
with `maxCompulsory = floor(fullRates[0].employeeRate × annualWageCeiling)`; `source: 'override'`; the wage
breakdown fields and `monthlyEmployeeRates` are still reported from the auto-calculation; `employerContribution = 0`. If the override exceeded
`maxCompulsory`, emit `CPF_OVERRIDE_ABOVE_MAX`. For foreigners the override is ignored (contribution 0,
`source: 'none'`).

**Known approximations (documented in the UI):** contributions are computed month by month on ordinary wages with
the employee share rounded down each month; real payslips combine OW and AW in the month paid and can differ by a
few dollars. The override exists so users can enter the exact figure from payslips or the CPF statement.

### 6.2 Income

```
employmentIncome   = monthlyOrdinaryWage × monthsWorked + additionalWages
totalIncome        = max(0, employmentIncome − employmentExpenses) + otherTaxableIncome
donationDeduction  = donations × donationDeductionMultiplier
assessableIncome   = max(0, totalIncome − donationDeduction)
```

### 6.3 Reliefs and the overall cap

For existing contributions `e` and additional contributions `a` (per lever), with the lever's statutory cap for the
profile (§6.5):

```
earnedIncome   = min(EIR(ageAtYearEnd), max(0, employmentIncome − employmentExpenses))
                 EIR: below55 if age < 55; age55to59 if 55 ≤ age ≤ 59; age60plus if age ≥ 60
cpf            = employeeContribution (§6.1)
srs            = min(e.srs + a.srs, statutoryCap.srs)
cpfTopUpSelf   = min(e.cpfTopUpSelf + a.cpfSelf, statutoryCap.cpfSelf)
cpfTopUpFamily = min(e.cpfTopUpFamily + a.cpfFamily, statutoryCap.cpfFamily)
other          = otherReliefs
totalBeforeCap = sum of the six
totalAllowed   = min(totalBeforeCap, personalReliefCap)
lostToCap      = totalBeforeCap − totalAllowed
chargeableIncome = floor(max(0, assessableIncome − totalAllowed))
```

**What a top-up amount means.** Every CPF top-up amount in the tool — entered as already made, or recommended — is
the part that can earn relief: a **cash top-up** to a Special, Retirement or MediSave Account (not a voluntary
contribution to all three accounts), to an account with room under its limit, and **excluding any part the
Government matches** (§5.4). The model keeps "one dollar in = one dollar of relief" for every recommended dollar;
whatever the giver adds to collect a matching grant for a parent is on top of the plan and outside it. The inputs,
the plan and `FAMILY_TOPUP_CHECKS` (§6.11) say so.

### 6.4 Tax

```
grossTax(ci)  = round2( Σ over brackets k:  rate_k × max(0, min(ci, from_{k+1}) − from_k) )      (from_{last+1} = ∞)
pitRebate     = 0 if rules.pitRebate is null, else min(cap, round2(rate × grossTax))
taxPayable    = max(0, grossTax − pitRebate)
marginalRate  = 0 if ci = 0, else the rate of the bracket with the largest `from` < ci
effectiveRate = taxPayable / totalIncome   (0 when totalIncome = 0)
```

`evaluate(input, additional)` returns the full `TaxComputation` for the profile with `additional` contributions on
top of `existing`. `baseline = evaluate(input, {0,0,0})`.

*Parenthood Tax Rebate.* PTR is a credit that offsets tax payable and carries forward until used. A dollar of gross
tax avoided therefore preserves a dollar of PTR for later years, so PTR does not change the value of a relief
dollar. v1 does not ask for a PTR balance; the UI notes that a user with unused PTR will pay less than shown this
year and that the saving is realised when the PTR balance would otherwise have run out.

### 6.5 Levers: eligibility, caps and room

| Lever | Statutory relief cap (`statutoryCap`) | Eligible when |
|-------|----------------------------------------|---------------|
| `srs` | `srs.capCitizenPr` for citizen/PR; `srs.capForeigner` for foreigner | `ageAtYearEnd ≥ 18` |
| `cpfSelf` | `cpfTopUp.selfReliefCap` | citizen or PR (has a CPF account) |
| `cpfFamily` | `cpfTopUp.familyReliefCap` | `residency` is listed in `cpfTopUp.familyGiverResidency` — citizens and PRs only: IRAS requires the giver to be a Singapore Citizen or PR (the recipient must also be an eligible family member) |

For an ineligible lever `statutoryCap = 0`, so an existing contribution to it earns no relief and raises no
warning. Then, per lever:

```
used = min(existing, statutoryCap)
roomIfEnabled = max(0, statutoryCap − used)
   cpfSelf:   roomIfEnabled = min(roomIfEnabled, levers.cpfSelf.roomToLimit)   when roomToLimit is a number
              roomIfEnabled = 0                                                 when roomToLimit is not a number
                                                                                and ageAtYearEnd ≥ 55
   cpfFamily: roomIfEnabled = min(roomIfEnabled, levers.cpfFamily.maxAmount)   when maxAmount is a number
room = roomIfEnabled if (eligible and enabled) else 0
```

**Room must be confirmed from age 55.** Below 55 the CPF Board does not accept a top-up above the Full Retirement
Sum, so a wrong guess about the room costs nothing. From 55 it accepts cash up to the Enhanced Retirement Sum while
relief stops at the Full Retirement Sum, and a top-up cannot be reversed; the people most likely to be given a
S$8,000 recommendation are also the most likely to have already set the full sum aside. So from age 55
(`ageAtYearEnd ≥ 55`, the age at which the Retirement Account exists) a blank `roomToLimit` means *not confirmed*,
not *unlimited*: the lever's room is 0 and `CPF_SELF_ROOM_UNCONFIRMED` asks for the figure. Entering any number —
including one at or above S$8,000 — confirms it.

`EXISTING_EXCEEDS_CAP` is emitted (once, listing every lever concerned) when an existing contribution is larger than
a non-zero statutory cap.

### 6.6 The optimiser

Because every relief dollar reduces chargeable income by exactly one dollar whichever scheme it goes into, the tax
saved depends only on the **total** additional relief. The optimisation therefore separates into (1) *how much in
total* and (2) *which scheme first*.

Let `CI0 = baseline.chargeableIncome`, `N(x)` = `taxPayable` after `x` dollars of additional allowed relief.

```
reliefCapRoom = max(0, personalReliefCap − baseline.reliefs.totalBeforeCap)                      (C)
floorCI(r)    = `from` of the lowest bracket whose rate > 0 and rate ≥ r  (∞ if none)
usefulRoom(r) = max(0, CI0 − floorCI(r))                                                         (U = usefulRoom(minSavingRate))
leverRoom     = Σ room over the three levers                                                     (L)
budget        = cashBudget if it is a number, else ∞                                             (B)

X = min(U, C, L, B)                      total additional contribution (a whole number of dollars)
```

Allocation: walk `preferences.priority`; give each lever `min(room, remaining)`.

```
plan.taxBefore = baseline.taxPayable;  plan.taxAfter = evaluate(input, contributions).taxPayable
plan.taxSaved  = round2(taxBefore − taxAfter);  plan.savingRate = taxSaved / X  (0 if X = 0)
```

**Binding constraint.** `'none'` if `baseline.taxPayable = 0`. Otherwise the constraint with the smallest value among
`U, C, L, B`, ties resolved in the order `usefulRoom`, `reliefCap`, `leverLimits`, `cashBudget`. (So with tax payable
and no scheme enabled, `L = 0` and the result is `leverLimits` unless `U` or `C` is also 0.) `minSavingRate` is
compared with bracket rates as ordinary floating-point numbers, so an input of `0.07` includes the 7% bracket.

**Unrealised saving.** `taxPayable(after X) − taxPayable(after min(U, C))`, i.e. the additional tax that could still be
saved at or above the user's minimum rate if the schemes or the budget had more room. It is 0 when the binding
constraint is `usefulRoom`, `reliefCap` or `none`.

**Why this is optimal.** Tax payable is a convex, piecewise-linear, non-decreasing function of chargeable income, so
the saving from each successive relief dollar is non-increasing. Taking dollars in order until the first one that
fails a constraint is therefore optimal for "maximise tax saved subject to: each dollar saves at least
`minSavingRate`, total ≤ budget, per-scheme limits, relief cap". The order of schemes does not change the tax
result; it only expresses the user's preference between flexibility and guaranteed interest (see §8.3).

**Statutory-rate note.** `minSavingRate` is compared with statutory bracket rates. In a year with a percentage PIT
rebate, the realised saving on low tax bills can be smaller than the bracket rate; `taxSaved` always reports the
realised figure and `PIT_REBATE_APPLIED` is emitted.

### 6.7 Savings ladder

The ladder partitions the range `[0, L]` of possible additional contributions (all enabled levers at their rooms,
ignoring budget and minimum rate) into tiers of constant saving rate, in the order the dollars would be contributed:

1. Dollars `[0, min(L, C))` reduce chargeable income from `CI0` downwards. Split this range at every bracket
   boundary crossed. Each piece is a tier with `rate` = that bracket's rate. Pieces in the 0% bracket (including
   dollars beyond `CI0`) have `rate = 0`, `zeroReason = 'belowTaxThreshold'`.
2. Dollars `[min(L, C), L)` exceed the relief cap: one tier with `rate = 0`, `zeroReason = 'reliefCap'`.

Adjacent tiers with the same `rate` and `zeroReason` are merged; empty tiers are dropped.
Here the tax after contributing `c` is `N(min(c, C))`, so the over-cap tier saves nothing.
`taxSaved = N(fromContribution) − N(toContribution)` (realised, so it reflects any PIT rebate),
`cumulativeTaxSaved = N(0) − N(toContribution)`, `usedByPlan = overlap([from, to), [0, X))`.

### 6.8 Simulator ("every extra S$1,000")

Step `s = preferences.simulatorStep` (default 1,000; minimum 100). Rows for contributions
`c_i = min(i × s, L)`, `i = 0 … ceil(L / s)`. For each row: `split` = allocation of `c_i` across enabled levers in
priority order (ignoring budget and minimum rate); `taxPayable`, `taxSaved = baseline − taxPayable`;
`stepSaving = taxSaved_i − taxSaved_{i−1}`; `stepRate = stepSaving / (c_i − c_{i−1})`;
`withinPlan = c_i ≤ X`. Row 0 has `stepSaving = stepRate = 0`. When `L = 0` the simulator is the single row 0.

### 6.9 Scenarios

Fixed comparisons on the same inputs. They ignore budget, minimum rate, priority and the `enabled` flags of `srs`
and `cpfSelf` (those are preferences) but respect eligibility, existing contributions and the user's room limits.
`cpfFamily` is included only when it is enabled (it depends on having eligible family members).

| id | Contributions |
|----|---------------|
| `none` | nothing |
| `srsOnly` | `srs = roomIfEnabled(srs)` |
| `cpfSelfOnly` | `cpfSelf = roomIfEnabled(cpfSelf)` |
| `srsPlusCpfSelf` | both of the above |
| `maxAll` | both of the above, plus `cpfFamily = room(cpfFamily)` |
| `recommended` | the plan from §6.6 |

For each: `taxPayable`, `taxSaved`, `savingRate = taxSaved / total`, and
`wastedContribution = total − min(total, usefulRoom(0), C)` — dollars that buy no tax reduction.
(For an ineligible lever `roomIfEnabled = 0`.) Scenarios are listed in the order of the table.

### 6.10 Monthly plan

```
deadline        = rules.contributionDeadline (31 December of the income year)
daysToDeadline  = whole days from today to deadline (negative if passed)
monthsRemaining = 12                         if today's year < incomeYear
                = 12 − month(today) + 1      if today's year = incomeYear   (current month counts)
                = 0                          if today's year > incomeYear
```

Schedule (when `monthsRemaining = n > 0` and `X > 0`): `n` rows from the first counted month to December. For each
lever with amount `x`: `base = floor(x / n)`, `extra = x − base × n`; the first `extra` rows get `base + 1`, the rest
`base` (whole dollars that sum exactly to `x`). `fullYearMonthly[lever] = ceil(x / 12)`, `total` = their sum; it is
reported even when the schedule is empty. When `today` is before the income year the schedule runs January to
December of the income year. The plan itself does not change after the deadline; only `DEADLINE_PASSED` is added.

Each warning carries a ready-to-display `message` and machine-readable `params`. At most one warning is emitted per
code, in the order of the table.

A message may quote a further saving only when the person can actually reach it, so it never quotes
`unrealisedSaving` (§6.6), which assumes room that no setting can create. `BUDGET_LIMITS_PLAN` quotes
`N(X) − N(min(U, C, L))`, the extra saving with the cash limit lifted; `LEVER_LIMITS_PLAN` quotes
`N(X) − N(min(U, C, B, Σ roomIfEnabled over the three levers))`, the extra saving with every scheme the person left
off switched on (§6.5: `roomIfEnabled` is 0 for an ineligible lever and respects the person's own limits). Each
sentence is omitted when its figure is 0. `FOREIGNER_NO_CPF` mentions the SRS cap only when `srs` is eligible.

- `TAX_RESIDENCY_CHECK` — "You worked here for half the year or less. You are a tax resident only if you stayed or

  pass valid for a year or more). Non-residents get no reliefs, so this plan would not apply."
- `SRS_NON_RESIDENT_WITHDRAWAL` — "If you have left Singapore when you withdraw, SRS is taxed as a non-resident's:
  at least 7.5 cents per dollar withdrawn at retirement age or under the 10-year rule for foreigners, and at least
  20 cents if earlier." When `lowValueAmount > 0` it adds " {lowValueAmount} of this plan saves less than 7.5
  cents per dollar today." `lowValueAmount` (in `params`) is the part of the plan's contribution that lies in
  ladder tiers (§6.7) whose rate is below 0.075.
- `CPF_SELF_ROOM_UNCONFIRMED` — "From age 55 the CPF Board accepts top-ups up to the Enhanced Retirement Sum
  ({ERS}), but only the part up to the Full Retirement Sum ({FRS}) earns relief, and a top-up cannot be reversed.
  Your own CPF is left out until you enter how much you can still top up with relief (S$0 if you have reached this
  year's Full Retirement Sum and your MediSave is full)."
- `AGE_55_PLUS_TOPUP` — "From age 55, cash top-ups go to your Retirement Account and earn relief only up to the
  Full Retirement Sum ({FRS} in {year}). The plan uses the room you entered ({roomToLimit}); check it against your
  CPF Retirement dashboard."
- `FAMILY_TOPUP_CHECKS` — "Family top-ups: the part the Government matches earns you no relief — the first
  {matchedRetirement} a year for a member on the Matched Retirement Savings Scheme" + (when the year has a MediSave
  figure) ", {matchedMediSave} for MediSave" + ". The amounts here are top-ups beyond that, to a family member who
  qualifies."
- `PARENTHOOD_TAX_REBATE` — "With unused Parenthood Tax Rebate your tax bill this year may already be S$0. This
  plan would then save no cash now: it makes the rebate last longer, so the benefit arrives in a later year."

Other wording fixed by the rule audit: `CPF_OVERRIDE_ABOVE_MAX` ends "Voluntary contributions to your three CPF
accounts earn no relief; a cash top-up to your MediSave, Special or Retirement Account does — enter it under
'Already contributed'." and `DEADLINE_SOON` ends "Banks stop taking same-day SRS contributions before midnight on
the last day, some as early as 7pm, and a CPF top-up counts on the day the CPF Board receives it, so transfer as
soon as you can."

`DEADLINE_SOON` mentions only the cut-off of a scheme that is in the plan: with no CPF top-up in the plan the
ending is the bank clause alone (`Banks stop … as early as 7pm, so transfer as soon as you can.`), and with no SRS
in the plan it is `A CPF top-up counts on the day the CPF Board receives it, so transfer as soon as you can.`
When `LEVER_LIMITS_PLAN` fires with no room at all and the person's own CPF is switched on but waiting for its
room (§6.5), its first sentence is `Nothing can be contributed yet: your own CPF is left out until you enter its
room, and no other scheme you switched on has room left.` rather than a statement that the schemes are full.

## 7. Engine API

```ts
// src/engine/index.ts
export function plan(input: PlannerInput): PlannerResult;                       // everything, one call
export function evaluate(input: PlannerInput, additional: LeverAmounts): TaxComputation;
export function computeCpf(input: PlannerInput, rules: YearRules): CpfComputation;
export function grossTax(chargeableIncome: number, rules: YearRules): number;
export function marginalRate(chargeableIncome: number, rules: YearRules): number;
export function sanitiseInput(input: PlannerInput): PlannerInput;
export function getRules(incomeYear: number): YearRules;                        // throws for unsupported years
export function defaultIncomeYear(today: string): number;                       // today's year if supported, else nearest supported
export function defaultInput(today: string): PlannerInput;                      // the worked example used as the UI's opening state
export const SUPPORTED_INCOME_YEARS: number[];
export * from './types';
// src/engine/facts.ts — the words of §5.4 with the year's figures filled in, shared by the page and the workbook
export function lockUpFacts(rules: YearRules, profile: FactsProfile): LockUpFactRow[];
export function transferCautions(rules: YearRules, profile: FactsProfile): string[];
export function lockUpHorizon(rules: YearRules, ageAtYearEnd: number): LockUpHorizon;
export const NOT_MODELLED: string[];
export const DISCLAIMER: string;
export const NON_RESIDENT_SRS_COST: { atRetirementOrTenYears: number; early: number };  // 0.075 and 0.2 (§5.4)
```

Requirements: no runtime dependencies; no I/O; no `Date.now()`; runs unchanged in the browser and in Node;
`plan()` completes in well under 5 ms for any input.

- `getRules` throws a `RangeError` unless `incomeYear` is a number that is a supported income year (the string
  `'2026'` and property names such as `'constructor'` are not).
- The rule objects (`getRules(year)`, and `PlannerResult.rules`, which is the same object) and
  `SUPPORTED_INCOME_YEARS` are deeply frozen; a caller that wants to change a rule must copy it first.
- `defaultIncomeYear(today)` reads `today` as §6.0 does; when it cannot be parsed the result is the latest
  supported income year whose rules are `final` (2026), never a projected year.
- `grossTax` and `marginalRate` sanitise their `chargeableIncome` argument like any numeric input (§6.0: not finite
  or negative → 0, then rounded down) and clamp it to S$140,000,000,000, the largest chargeable income that the
  clamped money inputs can produce (12 months of wages + additional wages + other income, each at most S$10bn).

`defaultInput(today)` is the brief's example: citizen, age 35, S$10,000 a month (S$120,000 a year), S$15,000 bonus,
S$5,000 other reliefs, nothing contributed yet, SRS and CPF (self) enabled, family off, no budget.

## 8. Web calculator

### 8.1 Principles

- **Answer first.** The recommendation and the tax saved are visible without scrolling on desktop and directly
  under a compact input card on phones. Results update on every keystroke; there is no "Calculate" button.
- **Opens in a working state** with the worked example, labelled as an example, so the first view shows what the
  tool does.
- **Show the working.** Every headline number can be traced to a line in the tax computation panel.
- **Plain language.** "Cash you lock away", not "illiquidity"; "tax saved per dollar", not "marginal rate" (the
  term is introduced once, next to the ladder).
- **Private by construction.** No analytics, no cookies, and nothing the user types leaves the device. The only
  network request is the optional Google Fonts stylesheet of §8.5, which the page discloses in its privacy note.
  Inputs persist only in the browser's `localStorage` (wrapped in try/catch; the app works without it).
- **Not IRAS, and says so.** A persistent, unobtrusive "Estimate only — not tax advice" line with the YA.

### 8.2 Layout

Desktop (≥ 960 px): two columns — inputs on the left (scrolls; a narrower rail from 960 to 1199 px so the result
tables fit), results on the right. Phone: one column in the order
*inputs summary → recommendation → ladder → plan details*, with a sticky bottom bar showing
"Save S$X · lock away S$Y" that scrolls to the recommendation when tapped. For a S$0 plan the bar reads
"Nothing more to put in this year" (or "Nothing to put in yet" when the plan is S$0 only because the own-CPF room
of §6.5 has not been entered); after the deadline it reads "Deadline passed · would have saved S$X".

**The date is live.** The page re-reads today's date when the tab regains focus or becomes visible and once a
minute, so a tab left open counts down, shows the last-day notice, and after 31 December switches to the
deadline-passed state (past tense, no instruction to transfer, an offer to plan for the next supported year).

### 8.3 Inputs

1. **About you** — residency (Citizen / PR / Foreigner tax resident), with one line for every choice saying what a
   tax resident is (at least 183 days in Singapore in the income year; §5.4); PR year (1st / 2nd / 3rd+) when PR;
   age at 31 Dec of the income year; birth month (only shown when the age is 55, 60, 65 or 70); income year
   selector (shown only when more than one year is supported; projected years are flagged).
2. **Income** — pay entry as monthly or annual salary (toggle); bonus and other additional wages for the year
   (commissions and incentives paid less often than monthly; those paid every month belong in the salary);
   months worked (default 12). *More options* (collapsed): other taxable income, employment expenses, donations,
   "Use my actual employee CPF for the year" (override).
3. **Already contributed this year** — SRS, CPF top-up to self, CPF top-up to family. Hints: include anything an
   employer paid into the person's SRS or topped up to their CPF in cash; for family, leave out any part the
   Government matched (§6.3).
4. **Other reliefs** — one total field, or *itemise* with the relief helper built from `rules.reliefCatalog`
   (spouse, children, parents, NSman, life insurance, other). The helper only sums amounts; it does not check
   eligibility and says so. A tick-box "I have unused Parenthood Tax Rebate" (`hasUnusedParenthoodTaxRebate`).
5. **Your preferences**
   - *Schemes to use*: SRS ✓, CPF top-up (self) ✓, CPF top-up (family) ☐. Per-scheme limits:
     "top-up that can still earn relief" for self — optional below 55, and **asked for from age 55** (§6.5): until
     it is filled in, the field is marked as needed, the plan leaves the person's own CPF out and says why;
     "most I would give" for family, with the matching-grant amounts in its hint.
   - *How far down the ladder?* A segmented control: a default preset named by its figure — **7 cents or more**
     per dollar for citizens and PRs, **11.5 cents or more** for foreigners (a foreigner who leaves pays at least
     7.5 cents per dollar to take SRS out, §5.4, so the 7% rung is a loss for them; the control says so and the
     person can still go deeper) — then **Top bracket only**, **Every tax dollar**, or a custom minimum. The
     labels state a figure, not a judgement ("worth it").
   - *Cash limit*: optional maximum to lock away this year. Because it decides whether the answer is one the person
     can afford, it is asked near the top ("How much cash can you spare this year?"), not at the end of the form.
   - *Which first?* **Flexibility first** (SRS → CPF self → family) *(default)*, **CPF interest first**
     (CPF self → SRS → family), **Family first** (family → SRS → CPF self). One-line explanation of each. No option
     is named for a guarantee: the CPF floor rate is stated with the date it runs to (§5.4).

Numeric fields accept digits and thousands separators (also a trailing "k", dots used as thousands separators and
full-width digits), show an "S$" prefix, select-all on focus, and clamp to their legal range with an inline
explanation (e.g. "SRS is capped at S$15,300 a year"). Switching *Other reliefs* between one total and *itemise*
converts a figure the person has typed in both directions; an untouched example total is **not** carried into the
helper: the helper opens empty, the example total stays counted and labelled as the example's until the person
enters something or ticks "I have none of these" (which sets S$0). Until the user has set them, fields still holding the example's values are
tagged "Example value", and the panel names which example figures are still in use.

   A one-sentence reason the plan stops where it does, driven by `bindingConstraint`

   S$12,400 of your income is still taxed at 11.5%." / "You have reached the S$80,000 overall relief cap.").
   When the recommendation is S$0, the card explains why and offers the *Every tax dollar* view.

   years. CPF: paid out monthly from 65, about 30 years. A MediSave top-up is not paid out: it can only pay for
   healthcare." for a 35-year-old, only for schemes in the plan. The age

   with the switched-off scheme on, or the cash limit lifted, and quotes that plan's own saving (never
   `unrealisedSaving`, which assumes room no setting can create).
2. **Savings ladder.** A stepped chart: x = additional contribution, y = tax saved per dollar; each tier a block

6. **How your tax is worked out.** Income → deductions → each relief → chargeable income → bracket-by-bracket tax,

("Get the Excel / Google Sheets workbook") under the card and again in the footer, so click-throughs can be counted by the
shop or a link shortener; without it the page shows no link. The page itself still records nothing.

A **Copy summary** button copies a plain-text summary of the plan (clipboard write inside the click handler, with
a select-the-text fallback). **Reset to example** restores the opening state.

A table that does not fit its container is restacked as label-and-value blocks (ladder, compare, tax bands) or
two-line rows (monthly plan); only the simulator scrolls sideways, with a visible cue. Print styles force light
paper, hide the controls and list the inputs used.

### 8.5 Design and accessibility

- Theme tokens on `:root`, dark values under `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` guarded by
  `:root:not([data-theme="light"])` and again under `:root[data-theme="dark"]`; `body` has an explicit background.
  No colour is defined only inside a theme block.
- Side gutters `max(16px, env(safe-area-inset-left/right))`; no horizontal page scroll at 360 px; tables scroll in
  their own containers; `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` for all figures.
- Every control has a label and a stable `id`; results region is an `aria-live="polite"` region; charts have a text
  alternative (the table); visible focus states; `prefers-reduced-motion` respected; colour is never the only
  signal (hatching/labels on the ladder).
- No external requests except an optional Google Fonts stylesheet with a full system-font fallback stack. The
  stylesheet is added by script after first paint, never as a blocking `<link>`, so a slow or blocked fonts host
  cannot delay or blank the page; headline figures must not wrap or clip with the fallback fonts.
- Visual direction: a calm, precise financial instrument — not a bank advert and not a government form. It must
  not imitate IRAS/CPF branding.

### 8.6 Build

`npm run build` → `dist/index.html`, one file with all CSS/JS inlined (Vite + `vite-plugin-singlefile`, Preact;
`build.modulePreload.polyfill` is off so the file contains no `fetch` call).
`npm run build:release` copies it to `release/sg-tax-optimiser.html`. `node scripts/build-artifact.mjs` writes
`dist/artifact.html`, the same page as an embeddable fragment (no doctype/html/head/body) for hosts that supply
their own document skeleton; it refuses to package a linked stylesheet or any network API.

## 9. Workbook (Excel / Google Sheets)

Generated by `npm run build:workbook` (`workbook/build.ts`, ExcelJS) from the same `YearRules` data.

- **Compatibility:** built for Excel 2016+, Google Sheets and LibreOffice. No macros, no `LET`/`LAMBDA`/`XLOOKUP`/
  dynamic arrays, and no `MAX`/`MIN` over an array expression; only widely supported functions (`SUMPRODUCT`,
  `INDEX`, `MATCH`, `MIN`, `MAX`, `IF`, `INT`, `ROUND`, `CHOOSE`, `ISNUMBER`, `IFERROR`, `DATE`, …), enforced by an
  allowlist test. All arithmetic is in whole numbers (tax in mills, CPF rates in millionths) so results do not depend
  on an application's floating-point behaviour. **Verified by recalculation in LibreOffice only**; opening the file in
  Excel and Google Sheets is a manual release step with a checklist in `workbook/README.md`.
- **Sheets** (the Planner header and banner say "PROJECTED RULES" when the year's status is `projected`)
  1. `Start` — what it is, three-step instructions, disclaimer, version and YA.
  2. `Planner` — inputs (clearly styled input cells with data validation and dropdowns) on the left; the
     recommendation, tax before/after, saving, binding-constraint sentence and warnings on the right.
  3. `Ladder` — the savings ladder tiers, a chart (written into the file as standard chart parts), and in-cell bars
     as a fallback where the chart is not drawn.
  4. `Scenarios` — the scenario comparison.
  5. `Simulator` — tax and saving for every extra step (default S$1,000) up to the maximum contribution; the table is
     sized from the rules so the smallest step (S$100) still reaches the largest room.
  6. `Monthly Plan` — instalment schedule to December from an "as at" date (default `=TODAY()`; a cleared or
     non-date cell also means today, where the engine would fall back to 1 January).
  7. `Tax Working` — the full computation, baseline and optimised side by side.
  8. `Before You Transfer` — the same lock-up facts and cautions as the web page, from `lockUpFacts` and
     `transferCautions` (§7), following the residency, age and as-at date on the Planner. The paid workbook must not
     say less than the free page. Its sentences are fixed when the file is built, so a check line at the top appears
     if a figure they quote no longer matches an edited Rules sheet.
  9. `Rules` — the rate table, CPF rates and ceilings, caps, deadline, sources. Editable so a buyer can update it for a
     new year: tax brackets can be inserted or removed (the helper formulas locate their neighbours by position), the
     deadline follows the income year, and a check line — repeated at the top of the Planner — flags a tax table that
     is out of order or incomplete. A personal income tax rebate rate and cap (0 by default) are editable cells.
- **Same story as the page.** The Planner states the recommendation as the same sentence as the page ("Put S$X
  into SRS and S$Y into your CPF as a cash top-up. You save S$Z in tax."), shows the lock-up horizon (the
  Monthly Plan sheet carries the affordability note of §8.4, as the page's monthly plan does), uses the page's names for the schemes and options and its rounding, and never shows
  a "further saving" figure the person cannot reach. Salary can be entered per month or per year (a dropdown; a
  yearly figure is split exactly as the page does: twelve equal whole-dollar months, the leftover dollars counted
  with the extra pay). The "how far down the ladder" cell opens on the page's default for the residency chosen
  (7%, or 11.5% for a foreigner) by formula; a rate typed or picked over it stays, and a cleared cell means every
  tax dollar (§6.0). The banner names the example figures still in use until they are replaced. Internal codes
  (warning codes, constraint names) never appear on a sheet; Tax Working uses plain labels. The Start sheet shows
  the version from `package.json`.
- **Differences from the engine, by design:** only the three priority presets of §8.3 are offered; the employer CPF
  figure is not calculated; `EXISTING_EXCEEDS_CAP` is shown as one line per scheme; two workbook-only check lines
  (tax table, deadline year) come before the engine's warnings.
- **Parity:** for the same inputs the workbook's CPF contribution, chargeable income, tax before/after, recommended
  amounts per scheme and tax saved equal the engine's (to the cent).
- **Verification:** `tests/workbook.test.ts` writes a set of input profiles (more than 300, including every boundary
  in §6) into the workbook, recalculates each with headless LibreOffice, reads the results back through named ranges
  and compares them with `plan()` — figures to the cent and Planner warning lines word for word. It also edits the
  tax table inside LibreOffice (rows inserted and removed) and compares the shipped release file with a fresh build.
  The parity tests are skipped with a clear message when `soffice` is not installed.

## 10. Verification strategy

| Layer | What it proves | Where |
|-------|----------------|-------|
| Unit tests | Each module matches §6 on hand-computed cases and edge cases | `tests/engine/*.test.ts`, `tests/review/*.test.ts`, `tests/web/*.test.ts` |
| Official worked examples | Engine reproduces IRAS / CPF Board published numbers (Appendix B) and this spec's worked examples (Appendix A) | `tests/engine/appendixA.test.ts`, `tests/engine/appendixB.test.ts` |
| Property tests (fast-check) | Invariants hold for arbitrary inputs: tax is monotone in chargeable income and in other taxable income (not in wages: under §6.1 and §6.3 one more dollar of a very low wage can add two dollars of relief — Earned Income Relief limited to earned income, plus the phase-in CPF share — and so lower the tax by a few cents); plan respects every cap, room and budget; no recommended dollar saves less than the minimum rate; one more dollar would either break a constraint or save less than the minimum; `recommended` saves at least as much as any feasible allocation found by brute force on small grids; ladder and simulator totals reconcile with `plan` | `tests/properties.test.ts` |
| Differential test | An independent Python implementation written only from this spec agrees with the engine on thousands of random inputs | `reference/sgtax_reference.py`, `tests/differential.test.ts` |
| Workbook parity | Spreadsheet formulas equal the engine | `tests/workbook.test.ts` |
| Browser end-to-end | The built single file loads with no console errors, shows the engine's numbers for the example, reacts to input, and has no horizontal overflow at phone width in both themes | `tests/e2e/` (Playwright) |

`npm test` runs everything except the browser test; `npm run test:e2e` runs the browser test against `dist/index.html`.
The differential and workbook-parity layers skip themselves (with a console message) when `python3`, LibreOffice
(`soffice` on `PATH`) or the `uno` Python module (package `python3-uno`, used to edit the workbook inside
LibreOffice) is missing; `npm run test:release` (`SGTO_STRICT=1`) turns a missing tool, or a shipped workbook
without calculated results, into a failure, so a release run cannot be green without every layer having run. It
also fails when `release/sg-tax-optimiser.html` is not the current build of `src/web`.

## 13. Updating for a new year

Rule values live in **three** places on purpose — the engine, and two independent copies that exist to catch a
mistake in it. A new year means updating all three, or the new year silently gets no cross-checking.

1. Update §5 of this spec with the new figures and their sources.
2. Copy the latest rules file (now `src/engine/rules/2027.ts`) to the new year and update the values (tax table, CPF ceilings and rates,
   retirement sums, BHS, matched top-up amounts, caps, deadline, sources); mark `status: 'projected'` until every
   value is confirmed. Review `src/engine/rules/shared.ts` (relief catalogue amounts, PR rate tables, source links)
   and the dated sentences in `src/engine/facts.ts` (the SRS retirement-age history, the interest floor's end date).
3. Register the year in `src/engine/rules/index.ts`.
4. Mirror the values in `reference/sgtax_reference.py` (its own copy) and in `tests/support/specModel.ts` (its own
   copy, plus its list of years); add the year to the generators in `tests/support/generators.ts` (the `year`
   recipe and `supportedYear`), or no random input will ever use it; and update every test that lists the years
   (`grep -rn "2025, 2026, 2027" tests reference`): some fail when a year is added, others pass and simply leave
   the new year untested.
5. When the **primary** year rolls over (for example 2026 → 2027): the example and default year follow
   `defaultIncomeYear`, but check the year wording in `src/web/text.ts`, `DEFAULT_INCOME_YEAR` in
   `workbook/build.ts`, and the release file name (`SG-Tax-Optimiser-YA<year>.xlsx`) wherever README, the tests and
   `release/BUYER-GUIDE.md` name it.
6. Run `npm run build:release` and `npm run build:workbook -- --year <year>`, then `npm run test:release` and
   `npm run test:e2e` (the strict tests compare the release files with a fresh build, so build first); then open
   the workbook in Excel and Google Sheets (`workbook/README.md`).

Citizen, age 35, S$10,000 a month for 12 months, S$15,000 bonus, S$5,000 other reliefs, nothing contributed yet.
