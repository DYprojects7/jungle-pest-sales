# Research log

How the rule values in `src/engine/rules/` were established. Compiled 13 November 2026.

## Method

1. **First pass** — one researcher per rule domain collected claims with a source URL, a supporting quote and a confidence level (`research-*.json`).
2. **Second pass** — an independent checker per domain re-checked the specific values the engine depends on against official domains (iras.gov.sg, cpf.gov.sg, mof.gov.sg, mom.gov.sg, moh.gov.sg) and returned a verdict per value (`verify2-*.json`).
3. Corrections from the second pass were applied to the rule data and the spec.
4. **Final audit** — once the product was built, an automated review of the model from a tax practitioner's point
   of view and a claim-by-claim check of every user-facing statement (229 claims across the page, the workbook, the warnings and the READMEs) were run
   independently (`audit-domain.json`, `audit-facts.json`). They found **no wrong rate, cap, ceiling or relief
   amount**, and three situations in which a recommended dollar could earn no relief or lose money: SRS for a
   foreigner who later leaves Singapore, family top-ups matched by the Government, and top-ups from age 55 above the
   Full Retirement Sum. Each is now handled in the model or stated beside the recommendation (SPEC §5.4, §6.3, §6.5,
   §6.11), and the wording corrections were applied to `src/engine/facts.ts`, the warnings and the relief helper.
   None of this was done by a qualified tax adviser: it is a careful automated cross-check, not professional advice.

### Limits of this research

- Official pages could not be fetched directly from the build environment (network policy), so every value was read through web-search extracts of those pages rather than the pages themselves. Quotes marked as paraphrases are the search tool's rendering.
- The first pass was cut short by a search quota and its own verification stage did not run. The second pass (`verify2-*.json`) replaced it.
- Negative findings ("no rebate announced", "no change in Budget 2026") cannot be quoted; they are recorded as unverified rather than confirmed.
- **Before selling or relying on this tool, read the primary pages listed under Sources in the app and confirm the values in the table below.**

Additional findings:

- 250% donation deduction is extended to 31 Dec 2029 (Budget 2026), so the projected year 2027 (YA2028) should also use 250%, not 100%.
- Paper returns (Form P, B, B1, M) are due 18 April from YA2025, aligned with e-filing; remove any 15 April reference.
- Two post-Budget support packages for the Middle East situation (Ministerial Statement 7 Apr 2026; second package about 29 Jul 2026) raised the YA2026 Corporate Income Tax rebate from 40% to 50% (minimum $2,000, cap $40,000). Household help was vouchers, U-Save rebates and cash payments. No personal income tax rebate appeared in either package.
- CPF Cash Top-up Relief: from YA2026 (top-ups made from 1 Jan 2025), cash top-ups that attract a Matched Retirement Savings Scheme matching grant do not qualify for relief. Relief for other top-ups remains up to $16,000 a year. The calculator should exclude MRSS-matched top-ups.
- Course Fees Relief has lapsed from YA2026 and Foreign Domestic Worker Levy Relief lapsed from YA2025; neither should be offered for the 2025, 2026 or 2027 income years.
- Dependant income threshold for dependant-related reliefs (including CPF cash top-up recipients) rose from $4,000 to $8,000 from YA2025.
- Working Mother's Child Relief is a fixed amount for children born on or after 1 Jan 2024: $8,000 first child, $10,000 second, $12,000 third and subsequent. Parent Relief for YA2026 is $9,000 per dependant if living with the parent, $5,500 if not.
- Reliefs above the $80,000 cap are disregarded and cannot be transferred or carried forward.
- The Finance (Income Taxes) Bill 2026 includes a broad-based Fixed Expense Deduction Ratio for self-employed persons and individual sole proprietors (a non-Budget amendment). Details and the effective YA were not retrieved; check this if the calculator handles self-employed income.
- Tax residency for foreigners has more routes than the 183-day test: a continuous work period straddling two calendar years totalling at least 183 days, and a work pass valid for at least one year. Employment of 60 days or less in a calendar year may be exempt for non-residents.
- The IRAS $250,000 sample taxpayer is Mr Fong, aged 64, with personal reliefs of $15,650; a back-test using age 35 and $1,000 EIR will not reproduce $234,100.
- 18 April 2027 falls on a Sunday. IRAS kept 18 April 2026 (a Saturday) unchanged, but no YA2027 filing notice was found yet.
- Not checked in this run and still candidate-only: SRS contribution caps, the 1-month payment deadline after the NOA, the 12 interest-free GIRO instalments, Earned Income Relief amounts for persons with disabilities, and any rounding of chargeable income.

- IRAS also excludes from employee CPF relief: contributions made during an overseas secondment or posting, and contributions on Additional Wages above the cap where the employee works for two or more related employers.
- IRAS: the $80,000 cap applies to the total of all reliefs per Year of Assessment, including compulsory and voluntary CPF; excess relief is disregarded and cannot be carried forward.
- The CPF rate PDFs also carry F/G tables (full employer, graduated employee) for 1st- and 2nd-year SPRs, separate from G/G. - The SPR tables show maximum employee shares of $400 (1st year, 5%) and $1,200 (2nd year, 55 and below, 15%) for 2026, consistent with the $8,000 OW ceiling.
- Age-band rule detail: on the birthday the employee is treated as having just turned that age and stays in the old band for the whole birthday month (CPF example: 55th birthday 13 Jan 2026 -> new rates from 1 Feb 2026).
- 2027: employers get a CPF Transition Offset equal to half of the 2027 increase in employer contributions, applied automatically. The extra 2027 contributions for ages above 55 to 65 go to the Retirement Account up to the Full Retirement Sum, then to the Ordinary Account. Neither affects the employee's tax.

Additional findings:

- Disability variants are exempt from the dependant-income test: IRAS says the S$8,000 condition is not applicable to Spouse Relief (Disability) or Parent Relief (Disability), and the CPF Cash Top-up page says the same for a spouse or sibling with disability. - A Fixed Expense Deduction Ratio for self-employed persons and individual sole proprietors is in the Finance (Income Taxes) Bill 2026. The search tool reported it as optional, 20% of revenue, for annual revenue up to S$50,000 (Clause 13(2)). The effective YA and whether it is a Budget 2026 or periodic-review measure were not verified. It is a deduction against trade income, not a personal relief, but it matters if the calculator supports self-employed users.
- WMCR has two caps beyond the S$50,000 per-child cap: total WMCR across all children is limited to 100% of the mother's earned income, and QCR/Child Relief (Disability) is allowed first with WMCR limited to the remaining balance.
